The pilot is starting up and has no results yet. This page sums up the first clarifications, from an architecture meeting on 29 October and a meeting with Demokratiløftet on 30 October 2026.

## The architecture meeting on 29 October

**The difference between Pilot 6 and Pilot 7.** The main difference is how the communication takes place. [Pilot 6](/en/pilotering/pilot-6/) is about one-way communication, while Pilot 7 is about two-way communication. Pilot 7 leaves room for less formal and more flexible exchange of information, and for more user groups, such as pupils.

**Possible shared solutions.** Dialogporten was discussed as a possible common component. KS Digital is working on secure chat, and this should be seen together with the pilot. It may also be possible to reuse legal assessments from other solutions and apps for communication between school and home.

**Need to narrow the scope.** Case 11 covers many levels and issues, and the scope is large. It may be wise to split it up. The pilot could start with a foundation that different apps and solutions can build on, rather than solving all the needs at once.

**Level of ambition.** Questions were raised about how realistic the deliverable is as it is described today. A common protocol could be a way forward, making communication between different solutions possible. It is uncertain whether suppliers will adopt and follow such a protocol.

## The meeting with Demokratiløftet on 30 October

Demokratiløftet explores what role digital public goods can play in a more coherent and democratic digital infrastructure, because more and more democratic processes take place in digital media. The project targets young people aged 13–18 and is in a preliminary phase, with Simula Met as technical partner.

The idea is not one solution, but an infrastructure where communication takes place in a larger ecosystem based on common components. The organisations, not the individuals, choose which tools from a shared toolbox they want to use. Among other things, the project is looking at:

- the European Social Stack as inspiration for a shared digital infrastructure
- the Matrix protocol as a possible technical building block for communication (the Norwegian Armed Forces use it)
- Spaces in EuroSky
- building on European digital infrastructure

The meeting also pointed out that Norway can use its room for manoeuvre to stay ahead of upcoming European regulation, such as the Data Governance Act. It must also be examined what the regulation means for the EFTA countries. Public procurement can be used more actively, by setting clearer requirements. This ties in with the work package on common supplier requirements.

The Norwegian Olympic and Paralympic Committee and Confederation of Sports (Norges idrettsforbund) is working on many of the same issues and could become an important driver of digitalisation for children and young people. Statped is also interested in Demokratiløftet.

## The way forward

- narrow the scope of the pilot and agree on what it should achieve
- examine which existing solutions and assessments can be reused, including the work in KS Digital
- see how the experience from Demokratiløftet can be linked to common components and regulation, and look more closely at the European Social Stack, Matrix and EuroSky
- assess how public procurement can promote a shared digital infrastructure
- contact Norges idrettsforbund, and ask what the Norwegian Armed Forces are doing with Matrix

The pilot has also received input from Digdir: a concept for a combined overview where parents and guardians see tasks, messages and information about the child from several organisations in one place.
